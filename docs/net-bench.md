# The measurement of HTTP on stafeto against Linux (`cargo xtask net-bench`)

A manual measurement on an Apple Silicon Mac (stage P7): ci has neither HVF nor VZ, so it builds the command and runs its host tests. The guests are the Linux guest of [linux-guest.md](linux-guest.md) and stafeto's image of the measurement (`boot-net-bench.img`), each on Apple VZ with four CPUs and 1 GiB, one guest at a time. The Linux guest is built on the Mac from pinned Alpine packages and never ships.

## What it needs

- `brew install vegeta wrk nginx iperf3 nghttp2` (vegeta 12.13.0 at a fixed rate, wrk 4.2.0 for the ceiling, Homebrew's nginx for the floor, iperf3 for the limit of the NAT, h2load of nghttp2 for W4 and W5). The versions go into the report.
- The Local Network permission for the terminal, as for `nginx-bench --machine vz`.
- No quiet Mac. Other work may run on it; the method below tolerates that and records it.

## The rows and the loads

| row | system | nginx workers |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Linux 6.18 (Alpine 3.24), nginx 1.30.6 | 1 |
| L4 | the same | 4 |
| S4 | stafeto, `netd`, nginx 1.30.6 | 4 |
| F1 | stafeto, `nginx-fast` 1.30.6 on the TCP/IP stack in its own process, on the second network device; `netd` keeps the first device and stays out of the data path | 1 |

F1 runs from `boot-net-bench-fast.img` (`cargo xtask linux-guest` builds nothing of it; `cargo xtask net-bench` builds it when F1 is among the systems). The image is the one of `cargo xtask net-vz --fast --nginx` with the table of the measurement (`table-bench-fast-vz`: the launcher of the shell on all four CPUs, `netd` on CPU 0, nothing else changed), the files of S4's image and the second device's static address `192.168.64.250` in `netd.conf`. The load goes to that address. The worker is one, so its pair is L1 (a worker against a worker); the tables also print F1 over L4 and over S4. F-Stack's own settings (`ports/f-stack/fstack.ini`) are in the report.

F1 has its own file of settings, `tools/nginx/nginx-fast.conf`. It differs from `nginx.conf` in the allowed directives (`user`, `worker_processes`, `worker_connections`, `worker_shutdown_timeout`, `reuseport`), in the three types written in the file (the other rows include `mime.types`) and in the path of the pid file; a host test lists the differing lines. The fingerprint of `nginx-fast -T -c /etc/nginx/nginx-fast.conf` is checked against that file, the files under `/www` against the same list as the other rows. The mmap room of a process (32 MiB, stafeto-design#598) limits the stack's worker: 200 connections on pages of 10 KiB and 100 on pages of 64 KiB pass, 200 on 64 KiB do not (the worker exits, the device goes back to `netd`), so a load with larger answers can end the guest of F1, and the step is then recorded as a failure of the guest.

| load | what |
|---|---|
| W1 | 900 files of 10 KiB (`/s/0000.bin` to `/s/0899.bin`, taken in turn), 64 long connections |
| W2 | 64 files of 256 KiB (`/l/00.bin` to `/l/63.bin`), 64 long connections |
| W3 | the files of W1, a new connection for each request (up to 64 at once) |
| W4 | the files of W1 over TLS 1.3 and HTTP/2, 64 long connections with up to 100 streams each (`h2load`) |
| W5 | the files of W1 over TLS 1.3, a new connection (a new handshake, no resumption) for each request (`h2load`) |

Both guests carry the same files and the same settings (`tools/nginx/nginx.conf`); `linux-guest check` proves it, and every guest the run starts passes the same fingerprints of `nginx -T`, of `/www` and of the versions before it is loaded. L1 differs from L4 in `worker_processes`, an allowed difference.

W4 and W5 take the settings with the server of TLS (`tools/nginx/tls.conf`, TLS 1.3 alone, HTTP/2, the key and certificate of the probe CA): `nginx-tls.conf` for L1, L4 and S4, `nginx-fast-tls.conf` for F1, each the plain settings with one more line, the `include` of `tls.conf` before the server of HTTP. A guest that runs them starts from the plain settings, passes the fingerprints of the TLS settings (`nginx -T -c`, which prints the three files of the settings) and restarts nginx on them (Linux) or starts it on them (stafeto); the first file is fetched over TLS from the Mac with `curl --http2` and compared with its source. The Linux guest has the files in its overlay, so `cargo xtask linux-guest build` rebuilds its initramfs with them (the hash of the image changes, nothing of the plain run does). The floor is the same Homebrew nginx with a second server of TLS on a port of its own.

`h2load` is an open loop at a fixed rate, and it writes the start and the length of every request to a file (`--log-file`); the percentiles come from that file, from the start of a request to the end of its answer. The correction for the lag does not apply to them: it reads the requests against one clock, and `h2load` has a timer for each of its 64 connections, each starting when its handshake ends, so the requests of the connections meet and part by a millisecond or two with nothing stalled (counted against one schedule `t0 + n / rate` over the requests sorted by their start, the lag was 1 to 3 ms even on the floor). The floor is the same `h2load` on the loopback and takes that noise in the rule of the noise; what the floor cannot take is a stall of `h2load` in a run of a guest, so the largest gap between the starts of two neighbouring requests, less four intervals of the rate, stands in the place of the p99 of lag (reliable with one thread, up to 16 000 a second; with two it is a lower bound): above 0.5 ms the step is marked "generator", and a failure that stall explains is noise. W4 opens its 64 connections one after another a request interval apart (`-r` and `--rate-period` in whole milliseconds, so a rate above 1 000 a second opens several connections in a millisecond), each connection at one 64th of the rate (`--rps`), so the requests of the 64 timers do not arrive in a burst. W5 opens a connection at the rate and sends one request on it (`-c` of all requests, `-m1`). The log starts a request after the handshake, so W5's latency is that of the request on a new connection and leaves the handshake out; the summary of `h2load` has the time for connect of the run (the console output of every step is kept). The ceiling of W4 is `h2load` with the connections open and one request in flight on each, as fast as the guest answers, as `wrk -c64` does for W1 (the criterion `-c100`; no warm-up of its own, so `/proc/stat` and the request count cover the same seconds), and W5 has none. With ten streams in flight on each of 64 connections the guest of F1 answers the first 640 requests and then none (the worker is alive and the CPUs are idle), so the ceiling uses one.

## The method

1. **Grid and walk.** One grid of absolute rates for all rows: 250, 500, 1 000, 1 500, 2 000, 3 000, 4 000, 6 000, 8 000, 12 000, 16 000, 24 000, 32 000, 48 000, 64 000, 96 000 a second (W2 and W3 start at 100: 100, 200, 300, 500, 700, 1 000, then the same). The walk goes from the bottom up, a step is a warm-up and a run of `--stage` seconds (default 10 and 60), and it ends at the first saturated step: below 95 % of the asked rate, p99 above 50 ms, an answer other than 200, or a line of nginx at crit. Before a guest's first step of a load, and when the guest sat idle for 45 s, one pass over the set at 2 000 a second (500 for W2) warms the open-file cache of nginx, so the low rates do not mix misses with hits.
2. **R\*.** The highest rate whose p99 is within 1 ms below the first saturation. Rates below R\* that fail 1 ms do not end the walk; they have a column of their own (the measure is not monotonic).
3. **Bisection.** Between the best rate L and the next point F of the grid, a probe at (L+F)/2, until (F-L)/L is at most 5 %.
4. **Correction for the lag of the load generator.** vegeta counts a latency from the moment a worker takes a request, and a pacer that fell behind sends the missed requests at once: its own stall is then in no latency. For each request the scheduled time is `t0 + seq/rate`, the lag is the time a worker took it against that, and the latency in every table is `lag + latency` (nearest-rank percentiles over all requests). The table of the report gives p99 as vegeta counts it and the p99 of the lag beside it.
5. **The floor of the Mac and the noise rule.** The same load on Homebrew's nginx on the loopback with the same settings and files, `--floor` seconds at a rate. It runs for a failing point of the walk and, in every round, at the three rates of the round. A step fails when p99 minus the p99 of the floor at that rate is above 1 ms and p99 minus the p99 of the lag is above 1 ms (a floor not taken counts as zero). A step with p99 above 1 ms that does not fail by this rule is noisy, and so is any step with a guest of VZ that came up beside the run. The marks "floor" (floor p99 above 0.5 ms), "generator" (p99 of lag above 0.5 ms) and "foreign guest" only describe a step. In the walk a noisy step is taken once more, and twice noisy leaves that rate undecided and the walk goes on; twice noisy in the bisection stops it with "at least L, above that the floor decides". A step that holds 1 ms is a pass whatever the marks.
6. **Rounds.** Confirmation and the runs at 50 and 80 % go in rounds, short and alternating. In round n the systems come in the order of a Latin square (round 1 L1, L4, S4; round 2 L4, S4, L1; round 3 S4, L1, L4), each started anew (and fingerprinted), so a slow drift of the Mac (load, temperature) falls on the systems alike. In a round, for each load of the system: the floor at its three rates, the ceiling by `wrk`, also for a row that has no R* (`-t2 -c64` and the criterion `-c100` (`-c200` for W2; 900 open files and 200 connections of one worker pass the 1 024 descriptors of a process), errors of connect; W3 with `Connection: close`), a confirmation run at R\*, and a run at 50 % and at 80 % of the R\* of the walk. R\* is confirmed by three passes in a row (p99 within 1 ms, one run per round); a run that fails, noisy or not, and a run that saturates (p99 above 50 ms from a single stall counts) steps R\* back by 5 % and counts anew, three steps at most, then R\* is "not confirmed" with all its numbers. The rounds go on for the open pairs up to 12 rounds, a limit of Mac time that the note does not have. A confirmation run is `--confirm` seconds (default 300) after `--confirm-warm`, stretched up to `--cap` seconds to reach 100 000 requests, since p99.9 is printed only from that many (below it the table has `-`).
7. **The CPUs of the guest.** `/proc/stat` per CPU before and after each confirmation run and each ceiling gives the idle share of each CPU and the CPU time per answer (Linux: everything but idle and iowait; stafeto: the same fields). A guest that prints fewer lines than CPUs leaves them unknown, and the table says so. "First limit": a CPU idle below 5 % at the ceiling is the guest's CPU; otherwise the NAT where the ceiling's transfer is above 80 % of the TCP limit of `iperf3`; otherwise the generator where a saturated step has a p99 of lag above 0.5 ms that is at least half of its p99; otherwise not determined.
8. **The limit of the NAT.** `iperf3` between the Mac and L4: TCP with one and four streams in both directions and UDP with 64-byte packets (packets a second sent), three repeats of each.
9. **The conditions.** For every step the Mac's one-minute load; for every guest the load and the other machines of VZ and QEMU at its start and end, its MAC and address, the fingerprints (`boots.tsv`).

The run promises no ratio between the rows; it gives numbers with the spread of their repeats. The rates of R\* are for this Mac on this day.

## The profile of the network service

`--image profile`, `--image pinned` and `--image cpu0` run S4 on `boot-net-bench-profile.img`, `boot-net-bench-pinned.img` and `boot-net-bench-cpu0.img`: the image of the measurement with `netd` built with the feature `profile` and, for the second and the third, other CPUs for the programs. In `pinned` the shell's processes, nginx's workers among them, stand on CPUs 1 to 3 (`BENCH_PINNED_CPUS`), so that every call of a worker to `netd` is a call on another CPU; the services of the image keep their CPUs of the SMP decision (CPUs 0 and 1) and `netd` stays on CPU 0. In `cpu0` every program but the process service stands on CPU 0, so that every call is on the CPU of `netd`. The difference in CPU time per answer between the two, at the same rate below the ceiling of both, divided by the calls to `netd` per answer, is the surcharge of a call on another CPU.

The counters (`services/netd/src/profile.rs`) are totals that `netd` prints as `net: prof <id> <name> <value> ...` records when `posix-net stat` asks; `net-bench` takes one snapshot before and one after each confirmation run and each ceiling and records the change (maxima since the snapshot before) in `cpu_profile`. A run on these images serves the profile; the ratio against Linux comes from `boot-net-bench.img`, which carries no counters.

Every confirmation run and every ceiling also records, for both guests, the CPU time per answer of each program (`cpu_procs`: user and system time from `/proc/<pid>/stat` between the two readings, the processes of one name together), the time of interrupts and softirqs per answer (`cpu_irq_us`, `cpu_softirq_us`: columns `irq` and `softirq` of `/proc/stat`, filled on Linux only when its kernel accounts the time of interrupts, and part of the busy time too) and the CPU each process of a name last ran on (`cpu_placed`, field `processor`). The readings of processes and of the profile come before the first reading of `/proc/stat` and after the second, outside its window; the warm-up of a confirmation run is inside the window of the CPUs and outside the answers it is divided by, which raises the figures by the share of the warm-up (10 % for 30 s of 300).

The file service built with the feature `profile` (the images with the profile of S4 and F1s, and the image of F1) counts its requests and prints `ramfs: prof <id> calls <n> reads <n> snaps <n>` at most every 100 ms while they change and at each notification of the service. `net-bench` appends the last record to each snapshot (`fs_calls`, `fs_reads`, `fs_snaps`) and records the calls and the reads per answer in `cpu_profile` as `fs_calls_per_answer` and `fs_reads_per_answer`. A worker that serves from the copy of the file it holds (the layer's cache of immutable files, `docs/posix-coverage.md`) makes none. `cargo xtask fast-probe nginx` checks it: 100 requests of a page the worker has served before make no read call (`STAFETO_FILE_CACHE=0` for the worker makes them).

On stafeto `/proc` lists the programs the process service started for the shell and what they started: `netd`, the file service and the other services are not in `cpu_procs`. The time of `netd` is the `ran_ticks` of its profile, and the share of the other services comes from the idle time of the CPU they stand on.

## The commands

```
cargo xtask net-bench run                      # nat, sweep and rounds for L1, L4, S4 and W1 to W3
cargo xtask net-bench run --systems l4,s4,f1 --phases sweep,rounds
cargo xtask net-bench run --systems l4,s4,f1 --workloads w4 --phases sweep,rounds
cargo xtask net-bench run --systems s4,l4 --workloads w1 --phases sweep
cargo xtask net-bench run --systems f1,f1s,s4,s4o --old ../sh4b2-old/target --workloads w6,w7 --rounds 3 --max-rounds 3 --ceiling 15 --warm 3 --phases rounds
cargo xtask net-bench run --quick --max-rate 2000   # a few minutes, to see that it works
cargo xtask net-bench run --resume target/net-bench/<time>
cargo xtask net-bench tables target/net-bench/<time>
```

`--stage`, `--warm`, `--confirm`, `--confirm-warm`, `--cap`, `--floor`, `--ceiling`, `--nat` set the seconds (the defaults are the full method: 10 and 60 for a step, 30 and 300 for a confirmation run); shorter values let the rounds be short on a busy Mac, and the report names the values used. `--rounds N` is the rounds that also take the ceilings and the 50 and 80 % runs, `--max-rounds N` the most rounds in all (default 12).

A run keeps one file per step in `target/net-bench/<time>/results/`, the output of every tool and the console of every guest in `raw/` (`seconds-*.csv`, one line per second of a step; the files of results of vegeta stay there compressed), and the tables in `tables.md`. `--resume` replays the records: a finished step is not run again, a guest starts only for a step that is missing. A step in which the guest failed (a line at crit, a console that does not answer) is recorded as saturated with the reason, the guest is dropped, and the next step starts a new one. The tables are Table 1 (NAT), 2 and 2a (R\*, the numbers at R\*, the ratios), 4 (common rates), 5 (ceilings), 6 (the floor), 7 (conditions), 8 (W3 by rate) and the effect of the correction.

`tables` recomputes the walk and the bisection from the recorded steps under the present rule (where a rate the search asks for was never recorded it prints what the run recorded and says so) and reads the confirmation from the first run of each round: the highest rate with three passes in a row, else the rate with most passes, with "passes of runs" beside it. The retreat process itself cannot be replayed; a new run confirms.

Two properties of the method. The Latin square is cyclic: it balances the places of the systems in the rounds and leaves their predecessors unbalanced (S4 follows L4 in rounds 1 and 2); every guest starts anew, so the effect is small. The lag of a request is counted from the schedule that the least late request sets (`lags` in `nginx_bench.rs`), the note says the first request; the difference keeps the lag from going below zero.

A run of the rows of stafeto alone (F1, F1s, S4, S4o) needs no Linux guest: without `--phases` the phase `nat` (iperf3 with L4) is left out, and `--phases rounds` takes ceilings alone. The workloads W6 (`/s/0000.bin`) and W7 (`/1k.bin`) are one file over long connections, a ceiling by `wrk` in each round and no walk of rates. S4o is S4 from another build (`--old DIR` names its `target` with `stafeto.img` and `boot-net-bench.img`), the reference of a change of the network service. Table 5a pairs the ceilings by round (F1s over F1, S4 over S4o, F1s over S4) and checks the bounds 0.85 and 0.95 of the acceptance of the switch, in every round and by the median.

Further options of a measurement session: `--switch-workers N` sets the workers of `nginx-fast` on F1s (the settings are a copy of the file with `worker_processes N`, the fingerprint is taken from the copy), and `--switch-pin` adds `worker_cpu_affinity`, which only records a mask and moves no worker (`docs/smp.md`): the placement of the workers comes from `--image pinned`. `--image profile` also builds the image of F1s with the counters of the network service, and the rounds record them for F1s as for S4. `--phases idle` lets each stafeto system stand 20 s with no load, three times, and records the change of `/proc/stat` and of the counters (`idle.<system>.<n>`, CPU time in microseconds over the whole wait). `--pause-after S` ends a run cleanly between two systems once S seconds passed, so that a command with a time limit can be resumed with `--resume`.

Workers on CPUs 1 to 3. The process service places a worker on the least busy CPU of the mask of the launcher that started the shell; with the plain image that mask has CPU 0, where `netd` and the switch run, and a worker that lands there is served in bursts and takes almost no connections. `--image pinned` gives the launcher CPUs 1 to 3 for S4 (`boot-net-bench-pinned.img`) and for F1s (`boot-net-bench-switch-pinned.img`, with the counters), so that workers made one after the other stand on CPUs 1, 2 and 3. `--s4-workers N` runs S4 with N workers (a copy of the settings with `worker_processes N`). On a pinned image every ceiling is judged by `broken_round`: the processes of the row's nginx after the master must be as many as the row says, none on CPU 0, and none may have used under half of the mean of the workers' jiffies or taken under half of the mean of the counter of the network service (`member0` to `member7` for S4: the SYNs each member of the SO_REUSEPORT group took, by position in the group; `cons0` to `cons7` for F1s: the frames of the wire the switch passed to each consumer). A broken round is kept as `broken.<key>.<n>` and taken again on a fresh guest, up to three times; the record of a round keeps `cpu_workers` (`name=pid:cpu:jiffies`) for each process. The master is the parent of the others (the numbers do not say: a slot that was freed comes back with a higher generation). Workers that share a CPU while another CPU after CPU 0 has none do not break a round, since each is served; the record names them (`stacked`). The master of nginx stands on CPU 1, which the services of the image share. Since the mask of the launcher leaves CPU 0 out, the process service does not count the mark of the master's own placement against its children (#677; a fork and then exec stays on the CPU of the fork when no CPU is lighter, since the parent of an exec is the process itself, and a parent the service never placed gets no discount; outside such a mask nothing changes), so three workers started in a row stand on CPUs 1, 2 and 3 (before, the mark made CPU 1 look busy and they stood on CPUs 2, 3 and 2).
