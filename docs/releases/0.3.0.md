The third release of stafeto brings the network: a TCP/IP service with sockets and epoll, a driver for virtio-net, DHCP, `wget` and `curl` over TLS, nginx on the system's own sockets, and `nginx-fast` on a user-space FreeBSD TCP/IP stack (F-Stack). It also brings the package format with signed packages and boot images built from packages, and the first steps of the EFI boot for EC2 Graviton. The kernel is still a small capability microkernel in Rust for AArch64; it boots in QEMU (`virt`) on any host, with HVF acceleration on a Mac with Apple silicon, and under Apple Virtualization.framework, on 1 to 4 CPUs.

## Try it

Download `stafeto-0.3.0-aarch64-virt-base.tar.xz` (the shell, the package tool and the base packages) or `stafeto-0.3.0-aarch64-virt-full.tar.xz` (every package, including the network programs; it needs 1024M of memory), unpack it and run `./run.sh` (`--cpus 4` for four CPUs, `--hvf` on a Mac with Apple silicon). `run.sh` gives the machine a network device with user-mode networking; `exit` or Ctrl-A X ends QEMU. In the shell, `wget` and `curl` fetch over HTTP and HTTPS, and `nginx` serves files.

## Highlights

- **Network service.** `netd` runs the TCP/IP stack (smoltcp) in user space on top of a virtio-net driver (`lib/virtio-net`, over virtio-mmio on QEMU and over PCI on Apple VZ). It speaks DHCP, ARP, ICMP echo, UDP and TCP over IPv4, answers DNS lookups for the resolver, and writes `resolv.conf`. Programs reach it through the POSIX socket calls of `libc.so`.
- **Sockets and events.** `socket`, `bind`, `listen`, `accept`, `connect`, `send` and `recv` on TCP and UDP; `SO_REUSEPORT`, `SO_LINGER`, time limits, `SCM_RIGHTS` passes of network sockets, `F_SETOWN` and `FIOASYNC`, `writev` as one send; `epoll`, `poll` and `select` over more than 64 entries; 1,024 descriptors per process. A buffer shared with `netd` carries the data without a message per call.
- **Clients.** `wget` over HTTP and HTTPS (BusyBox; TLS 1.2 only and no certificate check, see `docs/posix-coverage.md`) and `curl` 8.23.0 on OpenSSL 3.5.9 with the Mozilla root certificates.
- **nginx.** nginx 1.30.6 with PCRE2 runs on the shared sockets of `netd`, with TLS 1.3 and HTTP/2.
- **nginx-fast.** The package `nginx-fast` runs nginx on F-Stack (the FreeBSD 15 TCP/IP stack) in the process of the server, on a second network device that `netd` hands over or, in the switch mode, on frames that the switch of `netd` passes to the stack of the process. The stack runs 100,000 connections in the check `fast-probe`.
- **Offloads and CPUs.** Checksum offload and, as an option (`tso 1` in `/etc/netd.conf`), TCP segmentation offload on virtio-net in both directions where the device offers it (Apple's device does, QEMU's user-mode networking does not). `netd` has a switch thread and one stack instance per CPU of its mask, a session home per CPU, one port space and one shared address.
- **Step bounds.** Every kind of step of `netd` is at most 16,500 ticks under `-icount`, 10 percent under the bound of 18,369, and CI asserts it in the images that count steps. The kernel does not change for any of this.
- **Packages.** The `.stpkg` format, signed packages, the tool `pkg` (install, remove, upgrade, repo), the service `pkgd` that attaches a store from a disk, a disk service with virtio-blk, layers in the RAM file service, and the two boot images `boot-base.img` and `boot-full.img` built from package files. Releases carry a signed index; `docs/release.md` describes the keys, the series and the ABI fingerprint.
- **Other.** PCI on QEMU `virt`, a poll in the idle loop with a bell on the idle word of the target CPU, copies of immutable files inside the process (`pread` and the `mmap` fill without a call to the file service), `mmap` room to 1 GiB, hardened `crypt` and `/etc/shadow`, and a notice for every crate and package that ships.
- **EC2 Graviton groundwork.** An EFI loader, ACPI converted into a device tree, a GPT disk with an ESP, and the first-boot steps of `cargo xtask aws`. They are groundwork: this release is not an EC2 image.

## Numbers

Measured on an Apple Silicon Mac with Apple Virtualization.framework, 4 CPUs, `wrk -t2 -c64 -d15s`, the guest and the load generator on one machine, medians of six rounds in one session (pull request #318). They are single-machine figures from `cargo xtask net-bench`; ci cannot run them.

| Load | stafeto | Linux guest, 1 worker |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 KiB, `nginx-fast` on its own device (1 worker) | 189,109 requests/s | 164,183 requests/s |
| 10 KiB, `nginx-fast` on its own device (1 worker) | 53,898 requests/s | 115,921 requests/s |
| 10 KiB, `nginx-fast` through the switch, 3 workers, `tso 1` | 78,103 requests/s | |

At 10 KiB the Linux guest answers faster than `nginx-fast` with one worker; the switch with TSO and three workers reaches 78,103 requests a second at 10 KiB. The method is in `docs/net-bench.md` and `docs/nginx-bench.md`.

## Limits

- The network is IPv4. `wget` checks no certificate and speaks TLS 1.2 only.
- TSO is an option and is off by default; it needs a device that offers it (Apple VZ). On QEMU the checksums and the segmentation run in software.
- `nginx-fast` on its own device needs a second network device; its mmap room limits the worker to about 200 connections at 10 KiB (100 at 64 KiB).
- The file system lives in RAM unless a disk is attached; changes are gone after a restart.
- Graphics is a preview with one full-screen program, and there is no sound yet.
- Nine POSIX utilities wait for services of their own: `at`, `batch`, `lp`, `mailx`, `locale`, `localedef`, `gettext`, `ngettext` and `msgfmt`.
- Interfaces may still change inside 0.x. The series 0.3 is frozen in `packages/abi.txt`: a package built for it runs on every system of the series.
